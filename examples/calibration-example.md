# Worked examples — scale follows the failure

These are constructed judgment examples, not reports of executed product tests. They contrast decisions and proof limits rather than prescribing check counts.

## A moved documentation link

**Input:** “Fix this internal documentation link. Do we need another assurance layer?”

**Response:** Resolve the new destination and anchor against the actual tree; inspect the rendered link if rendering is in doubt. The consequence is a visible, reversible navigation failure. That bounded check addresses the edit. A new review framework adds no distinct evidence. If governing closure rules require a falsifier, an isolated broken destination should be rejected by the same resolver; no broad mutation campaign follows.

**Limit:** Link validity does not establish that the destination's substantive instructions are correct.

## Audio components pass but the command produces nothing

**Input:** “Every DSP unit test passes. Can we say the default listening path works?”

**Response:** Trace the actual default command and its assembly with controlled audio at the input boundary and observable produced blocks at the output. Empty successful output must remain distinguishable from unwired or failed processing. Preserve narrow DSP checks for their own claims; add the smallest assembled-path check exposing the missing wiring or emission prerequisite.

**Limit:** Producing a block establishes this path's liveness, not diarization quality, long-session reliability, or all device behavior. Broaden only for those promised decisions or an implicated shared cause.

## Same product, different consequence

**Input:** “It's just an audio tool; minimize testing of cleanup after export. Cleanup deletes the only original, and interruption can happen between export and commit.”

**Response:** Treat preservation through interruption as consequential despite the product category. Prefer a lifecycle that retains the original until the required content has been verified in a durably readable export. Exercise interrupted transitions and recovery with disposable recordings and independently known content. Observe the actual storage boundary; a mock acknowledging writes cannot establish persistence. Keep this protection reachable at the cadence its risk requires.

**Limit:** A local process-kill test does not by itself establish power-loss behavior on every filesystem. Preserve that distinction and any required further evidence.

## Consuming audits before deleting their source

**Input:** “Three audit reports have been reconciled into a plan. A checksum validates the plan file. Is that enough to delete the reports?”

**Response:** The checksum establishes bytes, not retention of meaning. Compare the source concerns with their enacted or canonically preserved dispositions, including qualifications and protected obligations. A copied-tree case that drops a material qualification can challenge an executable ledger check; independently inspect semantic preservation where the mechanism cannot decide it. Bind checks to the actual revisions. Retire the originals only when their durable value and continuing functions have been accounted for under the task's deletion authority.

**Limit:** An agent's completeness assertion or a validator counting rows cannot independently prove semantic equivalence. A useful unresolved concern remains active or explicitly deferred, not silently dropped by reframing.

## More reviewers, no new observation

**Input:** “Two agents approved the test plan from the same implementation summary. Should a third model approve it?”

**Response:** First locate what remains uncertain. If the concern is default-path assembly, observe that path; if it is a disputed criterion, derive it from owner requirements and actual use. A reviewer with different source evidence or a deliberately different scrutiny task may add value. Model diversity alone gives no fixed confidence multiplier.

**Limit:** Shared-source agreement is useful interpretation evidence, not independent execution or calibrated defect recall.

## Faster suite, apparent quality gain

**Input:** “The refactor is faster and the average score improved after we widened tolerances. One protected case regressed; the old recovery test has found nothing lately.”

**Response:** Assess the old and new implementations against stable, justified criteria. Report the tolerance change separately and preserve the protected case. Keep the recovery test unless its unique role is demonstrated redundant or legitimately retired. Reduce repeated setup or move costly unique evidence to an appropriate reachable cadence before deleting it. Retain the baseline until the replacement supports the relevant comparison.

**Limit:** Runtime and aggregate score do not establish preservation of the original obligations. Stop with that failure visible; do not call it a completed improvement.
